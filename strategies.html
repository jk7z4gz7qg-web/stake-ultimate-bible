Strategy 1: Martingale Betting System

The Martingale strategy involves doubling your bet after each loss to recuperate all previous losses and gain a profit equal to your original stake.

Mathematical Analysis

If you start with a bet of B, your betting sequence after several losses will be: B, 2B, 4B, 8B, etc. The cumulative losses after n rounds can be calculated by the formula:

Losses = B * (2^n - 1)

Practical Example

If you start betting $1 and lose five times, the total amount bet would be $1 + $2 + $4 + $8 + $16 = $31, meaning you will need to win a single round at $32 to cover all losses.

Strategy 2: Fibonacci Sequence Betting

This strategy uses the Fibonacci sequence (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ...) as a basis for betting, where you increase your bet size by moving one step forward in the sequence after a loss.

Mathematical Analysis

The total amount wagered after n rounds can be calculated using the Fibonacci formula, and the number of losing rounds influences your total risk.

Practical Example

If you start betting $1 and lose three times, your sequence would look like $1, $1, $2, subsequently betting $3, resulting in a higher total that requires careful calculation.

Strategy 3: D'Alembert System

The D'Alembert strategy suggests increasing your stake by one unit after a loss and decreasing it by one unit after a win.

Mathematical Analysis

The total amount bet can be tracked closely due to its linear progression compared to other systems, making it a more conservative option in risking your initial stake.

Practical Example

If you started betting with $1 and experience losses, your betting amounts will increase from $1 to $2 upon losing, etc., giving you a clear path toward risk management.

Conclusion

Each strategy has its own merits and risks involved. It's crucial to choose one that suits your playing style and limits.
